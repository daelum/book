# Chapter 6 — Measuring Your Starting Point

If you don’t record where you started, Month 12 becomes a feeling. Feelings lie. Side-by-side photos rarely do.

This chapter is not about becoming a spreadsheet vampire. It’s about collecting just enough truth that you can tell progress from noise — and know when you’re actually stuck versus merely impatient.

---

## WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

### The starter scoreboard (keep it simple)

You need four lanes:

1. **Training log** — exercises, loads, reps, sets. If you don’t know last week’s reps, you don’t know if you improved.
2. **Bodyweight trend** — not one dramatic weigh-in; a weekly average when possible.
3. **Girths** — at least waist at navel; optionally chest, arms, thighs.
4. **Photos** — front / side / back, repeatable conditions.

Weight alone is a blunt instrument. Combined with photos and measurements, it becomes useful. That’s the point: **stack cheap signals** until the story is clear.

### How to take photos that don’t waste your time

- Same time of day when you can (morning is ideal)
- Similar lighting
- Similar distance and pose
- No pump flex Olympics for “baseline honesty” shots (keep a separate “fun pump” album if you must)
- Wear the same shorts/top style each time

You’re not shooting a campaign. You’re building evidence.

### Optional extras (nice, not required on Day 1)

- Sleep hours (even a rough log)
- Steps / daily movement
- Resting measurements of mood/energy (1–5 scale)
- DEXA / InBody / callipers — only if accessible and you understand the error bars. Different tools disagree. Consistency of *method* beats chasing a magic device.

### When the data says “change something”

A useful decision rule from real training practice:

- Food adherence looks solid
- You’re recovering okay
- **But** training performance isn’t rising for a long stretch

…then it’s fair to ask: am I doing too much volume? Is stress/sleep the limiter? Is the programme a junk pile? Don’t answer with twelve simultaneous changes. Answer with one hypothesis.

Conversely: if you’re not tracking, you don’t get to claim a plateau. You get to claim a vibe.

### Pathway lens

| Pathway | Baseline emphasis |
|---------|-------------------|
| **A** | Weight trend upward over months + lift PRs + arm/shoulder photos |
| **B** | Waist + photos + lifts (scale may be quiet) |
| **C** | Waist + weekly average weight + lifts maintained/up |
| **D** | Audit current numbers honestly — including the lifts you avoid logging |

## WHY IT MATTERS

Beginners change programmes because they feel unchanged at week three. Often the photo comparison would’ve said: *keep going.*

Tracking also changes behaviour. People lift harder when the notebook is open. People eat more honestly when the app isn’t a performance for imaginary judges — it’s a tool.

And when life gets busy — construction hours, kids, travel — your log becomes the difference between “I still know my working weights” and “I guess I’ll start over forever.”

---

## WHAT EXPERIENCED BODYBUILDERS DO

- Log as they train, not “later tonight” (later tonight is a fairy tale).
- Weigh under similar conditions and think in **averages**.
- Take photos even when they don’t feel photogenic — especially then.
- Review on a schedule (e.g., every 4 weeks), not after every salty meal.
- Track calories and protein at least in learning phases; some later simplify once portions are calibrated.
- Use performance as a red-flag system: strength crashing + fatigue rising = investigate recovery and life stress, not only macros.

## YOUR ACTION PLAN

**Do this once this week — your “Day 0” package:**

1. **Photos:** front, side, back.
2. **Measurements:** waist at navel (tape snug, not theatrical); note bodyweight that morning if possible.
3. **Strength baselines:** pick 4–6 lifts you’ll keep for months (e.g., squat or leg press, hinge, bench or push-up progression, row, pull-up or lat pulldown, overhead press). Record what you can do with solid form.
4. **Pathway + goal reminder:** write them at the top of the log.
5. **Diet snapshot (7 days):** track calories + protein without trying to be perfect — just learn your current pattern. Honesty > aesthetics.

Then set calendar reminders: photos every 14–28 days; metric review every 28 days.

---

## COMMON MISTAKES

- Taking baseline photos in superhero lighting and follow-ups in a yellow bathroom bulb.
- Weighing after a big meal and calling it failure.
- Logging only the exercises you like.
- Tracking fifteen metrics and sustaining zero.
- Using measurements as self-harm instead of feedback.
- Changing the plan the night before review day because you’re nervous about the data.
