# Stage 5B — Calories, Macros & Tracking

Food is not a personality. It’s the metering system for fat loss and muscle growth.

If you lift hard and eat like the goal is optional, your body shrugs. If you obsess over meal timing while your weekly calories are chaos, your body also shrugs — just with more spreadsheets.

This stage is the nutrition system for *The 12-Month Body*: what calories do in STRIP and LIFT, how to set protein/carbs/fats without cult behaviour, how to track like an adult with a real job, and how to eat when the day is twelve hours long and dinner is whatever is left in the fridge.

Keep Stage 2’s sequence in your head. STRIPLINE decides the phase. This chapter decides the numbers and the logistics.

---

## What calories are — and how they map to STRIP vs LIFT

A calorie is a unit of energy. Your body takes energy in (food and drink) and spends energy out (basal metabolism, digestion, training, steps, fidgeting, stress, sleep debt, the lot). Over weeks, the relationship between average intake and average expenditure decides whether body mass trends down, holds, or climbs.

That is not a vibe. It is not “eating clean.” Clean food can still overfeed you. Dirty food can still sit inside a deficit. Food quality matters for health, hunger, training performance, and whether you can stick the plan — but the fat-on-the-midsection lever is still **average energy balance**.

### Maintenance

**Maintenance** is the average daily intake that keeps your trend weight roughly stable over a few weeks when training and steps are similar.

It is not one sacred number forever. It moves with:

- body size (bigger bodies usually burn more)
- muscle mass and training load
- daily steps / NEAT (non-exercise activity — pottering, pacing, standing, fidgeting)
- sleep and stress
- how aggressive the previous phase was

Treat maintenance as a **working estimate**, then let the scale trend and photos referee it.

A useful mental model: maintenance is a **range**, not a laser line. Two weeks at ±100 kcal around the true average often look like “holding.” That is why tiny daily obsessing is wasted effort and weekly averages are the grown-up tool.

### Why the scale lies short-term (and why you still use it)

Use the scale. Do not worship it.

Short-term weight shifts come from:

- **Glycogen + water** — carbs and hard training pull water into muscle; low-carb days dump some of it
- **Sodium and restaurant meals** — hold extra water without adding fat overnight
- **Gut content** — high-fibre days and big meals sit on the scale
- **Inflammation / soreness** — heavy lower-body sessions can bump morning weight
- **Hormonal cycle** (where relevant) — water retention patterns across the month
- **Stress and sleep** — cortisol/sleep debt can change water handling and hunger

Fat loss still shows up as a **downward trend** across weeks when the deficit is real. Fat gain still shows up as an **upward trend** when the surplus is real. Your job is to stop treating a single weigh-in like a court verdict.

**Practical weigh-in protocol:**

1. Same conditions most mornings (after bathroom, before food/drink, similar clothing).
2. Record the number without emotional commentary.
3. Average the week.
4. Compare week-to-week averages alongside photos and waist.

### Deficit (STRIP default)

A **deficit** means average intake sits below what you need to hold weight. Fat loss requires this over time. STRIP is fat-loss-first for anyone carrying meaningful fat or skinny-fat softness — so most readers start here.

Practical starting point for many busy adults:

| Phase intent | Starting calorie adjustment |
|--------------|-----------------------------|
| STRIP fat loss | ~**300–500 kcal/day** below estimated maintenance |
| Larger bodies / faster early drop (if hunger and training hold) | sometimes nearer **−500 to −700**, then moderate |
| Too aggressive (strength collapsing, misery, adherence dying) | raise calories; heroics are not a strategy |

Expect noisy weeks. Water, glycogen, salt, gut content, training soreness, and hormones move the scale more than fat does day to day. Judge the **weekly average**, photos, waist, and strength — not Tuesday morning panic.

STRIP still wants **high protein** and progressive lifting. The goal is not “smaller soft person.” The goal is less fat while you keep (and ideally improve) the muscle signal.

How aggressive should the deficit be? Aggressive enough that photos change over a month. Gentle enough that you can still train, sleep, work, and not hate your life. If your only personality trait on a cut becomes irritability and skipped sessions, you did not find discipline — you found a deficit that is too deep for your current recovery budget.

**STRIP calorie reality checks:**

- Early weeks often drop faster (water/glycogen). Do not assume that rate continues.
- If waist and photos improve while scale weight stalls briefly, you may be recomposing or holding water — stay the course before slashing again.
- If strength is free-falling and workouts are junk for two weeks, raise food or cut volume before you “add more cardio punishment.”

### Surplus (LIFT emphasis — when earned)

A **surplus** means average intake sits above maintenance so gaining tissue is easier. In STRIPLINE, this is not month-one cosplay for someone who still has a midsection story to finish. LIFT calories are earned when photos and adherence say the middle is ready enough — or when a true hardgainer exception was documented honestly.

Practical starting surplus for many:

| Phase intent | Starting calorie adjustment |
|--------------|-----------------------------|
| LIFT growth | ~**200–400 kcal/day** above maintenance |
| Impatient dirty bulk | usually buys extra fat once a modest surplus already covers growth |

Patience beats chaos. Soft ≠ failure if strength is climbing and you still have months on the clock. Aborting a growth phase at week eight because the mirror got slightly softer is how people stay forever small-soft and confused.

In a surplus, many people unconsciously move more (NEAT rises — pacing, fidgeting, general pottering). That means a chunk of “extra” calories does not always become fat one-for-one. Still: do not use NEAT as an excuse to eat like a hardgainer meme and wonder why nothing happens. Track steps so the silent changes are visible.

**LIFT calorie reality checks:**

- Aim for slow scale drift upward over weeks, not a revenge bulk.
- Keep protein high so the surplus has somewhere useful to go.
- If waist is racing ahead of strength for several weeks, trim 100–200 kcal.
- Appetite can be a skill — scheduled feeds and liquid calories help when chewing becomes the bottleneck.

### Near-maintenance / recomp

Some stretches sit near maintenance: skill-building weeks, transitions between STRIP and LIFT, or skinny-fat phases where protein, lifting, and steps do the quiet work while scale weight barely moves. Photos and waist still matter more than the decimal on the scale.

Recomp is not magic and it is not an excuse to avoid deciding. It is a useful holding pattern when:

- you are new enough that muscle and fat can move usefully near maintenance
- you are bridging phases without chaos
- life stress says “do not open a deep deficit or surplus right now”

If you have a clear midsection problem and keep “recomping” for nine months with soft photos, you are usually just avoiding STRIP.

### Quick map

| Energy setting | STRIPLINE job | What “winning” looks like |
|----------------|---------------|---------------------------|
| Deficit | STRIP | Waist/photos improve; strength holds or rises slowly; adherence real |
| Maintenance ± nudge | Hold / recomp / transition | Composition improves without big scale drama |
| Surplus | LIFT (earned) | Strength and size climb; fat gain stays controlled |

If you only remember one sentence: **calories set the phase; protein and training protect the look.**

### Why weekly averages beat daily drama

Fat loss and fat gain are slow relative to water shifts. You can “gain” a kilo overnight from a salty takeaway and glycogen refill without gaining a kilo of fat. You can “lose” fast in week one of a cut mostly from glycogen and gut content.

So:

- Weigh most mornings (or several times per week) under similar conditions if the scale doesn’t wreck your head.
- Average the week.
- Pair that average with waist and photos every 2–4 weeks.
- Ask: is the midsection story moving the way STRIP or LIFT intends?

If the scale is a mental landmine for you, lean harder on waist + photos + how work trousers fit, and use weight less often. The goal is decision-quality data, not a daily emotional flogging.

### NEAT — the silent calorie thief (and gift)

When you eat in a surplus, many people unconsciously move more: pacing, fidgeting, taking the stairs, pottering. When you diet hard, some people become quiet energy-savers without noticing — fewer steps, more sitting, less spontaneous movement.

That is why step targets matter in STRIP. They keep expenditure from silently collapsing while you cut food. In LIFT, endless cardio that erases a carefully built surplus is equally silly. Match movement to the phase instead of collecting random punishment sessions.

## Protein, carbs, and fats — what they do, and practical ranges

Macros are the three big energy nutrients: protein, carbohydrate, and fat. Alcohol is the awkward fourth that still counts.

You do not need a PhD. You need a hierarchy you will not invert.

### The hierarchy (memorise this)

1. **Average calories vs your needs** — deficit to lose fat (STRIP default), near-maintenance to recomp/hold, surplus when LIFT is earned.
2. **Protein** — high enough to support muscle in whatever phase you’re in.
3. **Carbs and fats** — fill the rest in a way you can digest, train on, and repeat.
4. **Micronutrients, fibre, hydration** — health and adherence glue.
5. **Meal timing / food “rules”** — useful seasoning, rarely the main meal.

Most beginners invert this list. They buy a fat burner before they know if they’re eating 1,800 or 3,800 by accident.

### Protein

Protein supplies amino acids for repair and growth. In a deficit it helps you hold muscle and stay fuller. In a surplus it still matters — extra carbs do not replace it.

**Practical daily band for resistance trainees:**

> **~1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of body weight per day**  
> (about **0.7–1 g per lb** as a simple high heuristic)

Notes that keep this sane:

- If body weight is very high, use **goal weight** or an adjusted body weight so the target is achievable.
- In STRIP, lean toward the **higher end** of the band when hunger and retention need help.
- In LIFT, still hit a solid total; surplus carbs are fuel, not a protein substitute.
- Distribute across **3–5 feedings** if you can. Perfect timing is optional; **daily total is not**.

Protein powder is convenience food, not magic. Matched grams from yoghurt, meat, eggs, or powder do the same job for hitting the number.

### Fats

Dietary fat supports hormone production, fat-soluble vitamins, cell membranes, and — bluntly — whether food tastes like something you’ll eat for twelve months.

**Loose practical band:** ~**0.6–1 g of fat per kg** of body weight as a starting floor/ceiling guide for many people. Do not crush fats to zero to “make room for carbs.” Do not flood fats so high that protein and training carbs get squeezed out of a modest calorie budget.

Choose mostly unsaturated sources you tolerate (olive oil, avocado, nuts, fatty fish) and keep room for foods you actually enjoy. Exact split can flex with preference.

### Carbohydrates

Carbs fill glycogen, support hard training, and are often the easiest lever for energy once protein and fats are set. Fruit, potatoes, rice, oats, bread, pasta — none of these are morally superior or evil. They are tools.

**Practical rule after protein and fats are set:**

> Calories remaining ÷ 4 ≈ carbohydrate grams for the day.

Bias more carbs around hard training sessions if performance feels better that way. On rest days you can keep carbs similar for simplicity, or nudge them down slightly if it helps hunger control in STRIP — consistency beats clever micro-timing.

### Putting macros into one picture

Once calories and protein are locked, fats and carbs are mostly preference + performance:

| Situation | Bias that often helps |
|-----------|------------------------|
| Hard lower-body / high-volume training days | More carbs around the session |
| Very low appetite in LIFT | Liquid carbs/milk, fruit, rice; keep protein non-negotiable |
| STRIP hunger loud at night | Save a satisfying protein + carb dinner; don’t “save” all food for 9 p.m. chaos |
| Digestion issues with high fat | Lower fat toward ~0.6 g/kg; raise carbs |
| Digests fats well, hates huge rice portions | Raise fats toward ~1 g/kg; carbs fill less |

There is no trophy for the most complicated split. The trophy is still hitting the weekly average twelve months from now.

### Micronutrients without becoming a supplement catalogue

Hit most of this from food:

- colourful plants across the week
- some dairy or fortified alternative if you tolerate it
- fish a couple of times a week when possible (or discuss omega-3 options later in the tools section of the book)
- iodised salt / normal varied meals rather than fear of every mineral

Supplements can fill gaps. They do not excuse a diet of three foods and hope. Creatine and basics belong in the tools conversation later; here, food structure comes first.

### Fibre, veggies, hydration

These are not optional “health fluff.” Fibre and high-volume vegetables make STRIP hunger manageable. Water makes everything less miserable. If your diet is only protein shakes and dry chicken with no plants, expect constipation, cravings, and a mood that belongs in a parking-fine queue.

A practical hydration default for many adults is drinking enough that urine is pale most of the day — more in heat, on site, or in hard training — without turning yourself into a water-chugging mascot. Pair sodium and fluids like a normal eater, not a chemistry experiment, unless a coach or clinician has you on a specific protocol **[PLACEHOLDER: coach-specific detail]**.

### Alcohol

Alcohol is energy. Rough guide many people use: ~**7 kcal per gram**, before mixers. It also lowers inhibition, wrecks sleep for some people, and turns “I’ll estimate” into “I forgot three drinks.” Budget it. Log it. Don’t build your identity around purity theatre *or* nightly obliteration.

If you drink, prefer a planned number inside the weekly calorie average over a surprise blackout of logging. Beer and sugary mixers add up fast; spirits with zero/low-cal mixers are easier to budget — still not free.

## How to set your numbers — step by step

No mysticism. Estimate → choose phase → run → review.

### Step 1 — Get a maintenance estimate

Pick one starter method (both are fine):

**Method A — calculator + humility**  
Enter your stats into a reputable TDEE/calorie estimator (or the metering tool in the next section). Treat the output as a **guess**, not a prophecy.

**Method B — reverse engineer from real life**  
Track intake honestly for **10–14 days** while eating “normally.” Average daily calories. Watch weekly average weight. If weight is roughly flat, that intake is near maintenance. If weight is rising or falling, adjust the estimate accordingly.

Many people do A to start, then correct with B within two to four weeks.

### Step 2 — Choose the phase (STRIPLINE decides)

| Your situation | Default calorie setting |
|----------------|-------------------------|
| Meaningful fat to lose / skinny-fat soft middle | **STRIP deficit** |
| Midsection ready enough; adherence high; ready to grow | **LIFT surplus** (earned) |
| Transition, skill block, or honest recomp stretch | **Near maintenance** |
| True hardgainer with low fat and poor appetite | Document the exception; often a patient surplus — not “dirty bulk because impatient” |

Write the daily calorie target in your logbook. Round to something you can hit (±50–100 kcal is normal noise).

### Step 3 — Set protein first

1. Choose body weight or adjusted/goal weight in kg.
2. Multiply by **1.6–2.2**.
3. Write the gram target.
4. Build meals that make that number boringly inevitable.

Example band for an 85 kg trainee: roughly **136–187 g protein/day**. Pick a number in that band you can hit on workdays.

### Step 4 — Set fats, then fill carbs

1. Set fats around **0.6–1 g/kg** (loose).
2. Convert protein and fat grams to calories (protein × 4, fat × 9).
3. Subtract from daily calorie target.
4. Remaining calories ÷ 4 = carbohydrate grams.

If the carb number looks tiny and you train hard, nudge fats down within the band rather than nuking protein.

### Step 5 — Run 2–4 weeks before rewriting your life

Change one big lever at a time. Hit the targets. Lift. Sleep enough to function. Walk.

Then review:

| Signal | Likely move |
|--------|-------------|
| Losing faster than ~0.5–1% body weight/week with strength crashing | Eat more |
| Scale stuck 2–3+ weeks, photos soft, adherence honest | Trim ~100–200 kcal or raise steps |
| Scale stuck but photos/waist better | Often stay the course |
| Soft photos + weight ripping up in LIFT | Trim surplus |
| Strength up, slight softness, still early in earned LIFT | Patience |

[PLACEHOLDER: coach-specific detail — exact weekly loss/gain rate targets, check-in cadence, and adjustment rules if following a particular coaching desk]

### Step 6 — Don’t ego-flip phases

STRIP fails when people “reward” three good weeks with an unplanned surplus weekend festival, then panic-diet harder. LIFT fails when people abort at the first soft mirror photo. Give the phase enough runway that the data means something.

### A one-page setup you can actually finish tonight

Open your notes app or workbook and write:

1. Phase: STRIP / hold / LIFT  
2. Estimated maintenance: ______ kcal  
3. Daily calorie target: ______ kcal  
4. Protein target: ______ g  
5. Fat target (optional if simplifying): ______ g  
6. Carb target (optional if simplifying): ______ g  
7. Review date (14–28 days out): ______  
8. Photo reminder set: yes / no  

If those eight lines are blank, you do not have a nutrition plan yet. You have a vibe.

## How to track — step by step

Tracking is literacy, not a personality transplant. You are learning what portions actually are. Most people are shocked in week one. That shock is useful.

If tracking food is psychologically rough because of a disordered eating history, get professional support and use a gentler method (portion frameworks, plate method, coach-guided structure). This book is not a substitute for that care.

### 1) Get a kitchen scale

Digital, grams, cheap is fine. Put it on the bench. Leave it there. A scale you have to dig out of a drawer will not get used on a twelve-hour day.

### 2) Weigh food the useful way

- Weigh **raw** meats consistently *or* cooked consistently — pick a method and stick to it so the database matches.
- Weigh calorie-dense foods that are easy to underestimate: oils, peanut butter, cheese, nuts, dressings, butter.
- Vegetables can be looser once you know your pattern; oils cannot.
- “A drizzle” is often two tablespoons. Two tablespoons is not a drizzle. The scale ends the debate.

**Raw vs cooked tip:** 100 g raw chicken is not 100 g cooked chicken. If your app entry says “chicken breast, raw,” weigh it raw. If it says cooked, weigh cooked. Mixing those up is a classic silent error that makes people think maths is broken when their method is.

### 3) Read labels without getting conned

- Check **serving size** before the pretty front of pack.
- Note whether numbers are per 100 g or per serving.
- Liquids and “health” bars still count.
- When cooking mixed dishes, weigh ingredients in, then portion the finished meal by weight if you need accuracy.

Example: a “healthy” granola can be denser than cake once you measure the bowl you actually pour. Front-of-pack marketing is not a macro entry. The nutrition panel is.

### 4) Pick one logging app and stop shopping for apps

Any decent food logger works if you use it. Search the database carefully (brand + exact product). Save frequent meals as favourites or recipes so busy days take thirty seconds, not thirty minutes.

Build a personal library in week one:

- your default breakfast
- two work lunches
- two dinners
- your usual shake
- your usual coffee milk amount

After that, most days are tap-tap-done. Tracking should become boring. Boring is the point.

### 5) Log as you go — especially the “harmless” bits

Coffee milk. Sauce. Weekend nibbles. The bite of whatever your kids left. Unlogged food is how people swear they eat 1,900 while maintaining at 2,600.

If you hate logging at dinner time, log earlier feeds during the day and only finish the last meal at night. The open loop of “I’ll add it later” is where entire evenings disappear.

### 6) Use weekly calorie averages — not daily panic

Daily intake will swing. Training days, rest days, social meals, travel. That is normal.

**Do this instead:**

1. Log daily.
2. At week’s end, average the seven days.
3. Compare average intake to your target.
4. Compare weekly average weight (or 7-day trend) plus photos/waist/strength.

One high day does not ruin a cut. One low day does not make you “disciplined.” Trends do.

A clean way to think about it: if your target is 2,300 kcal, a week that lands at ~16,100 total (~2,300/day average) is on target even if Wednesday was 2,800 and Thursday was 1,900. Chase the week, not a perfect straight line.

### 7) Rest days

You still need protein. Calories can match training days for simplicity, or sit slightly lower if it helps STRIP hunger — **[PLACEHOLDER: coach-specific detail]** on exact rest-day carb protocol if you use one. What matters is that rest days are logged, not treated as free space outside the dataset.

Rest days are also where “I earned a blowout because I trained hard yesterday” turns into quiet maintenance. Keep the structure. Keep protein. Keep walking if STRIP is the job.

### 8) Eating out — estimate like an adult

You will not weigh restaurant risotto on a date night. Good.

- Prioritise a protein main.
- Assume oil and sauces are more generous than home cooking.
- Log a best-estimate entry the same day (search similar meals; slightly overestimate rather than pretend it was steamed air).
- Return to precision at home. One estimated meal inside a tracked week still teaches you more than a month of “I’ll be good later.”

Fast-food rule of thumb: use the chain’s published numbers when available. Local café rule of thumb: pick a similar generic entry and add a buffer for oil. Pride about precise restaurant macros is usually fake precision. Directionally honest beats delusionally exact.

### 9) Alcohol budgeting

Decide before the first drink:

- How many drinks?
- What are they (beer, wine, spirits + mixer)?
- What food will you cut or keep to stay near the weekly average?

Log the drinks. If sleep and training quality fall apart every time you drink, that is data — not a moral lecture.

A simple budget method: keep protein hit, reduce carbs/fats earlier in the day by roughly the drink calories, and stop when the planned number is done. “I’ll see how I feel” is how weekly averages get ambushed.

### 10) When to simplify to calories + protein only

Full macro theatre helps some people. It makes others quit by Thursday.

**Simplify to calories + protein when:**

- tracking all three macros is killing adherence
- you’re competent enough that fats/carbs flex without chaos
- life load spiked (new baby, overtime, travel) and you need the minimum viable dashboard

Hit calories. Hit protein. Let carbs and fats fill the rest from foods you tolerate. That pair alone outperforms “I eat pretty clean” memory every time.

Simplifying is not failing. Quitting tracking entirely because you tried to micromanage every gram of fat on a fourteen-hour shift — that is the failure mode this rule prevents.

## Meal structure, default foods, and busy-day patterns

You do not need seven different gourmet dinners. You need repeatable feeds that hit protein without requiring a chef and a silent kitchen.

### 3–5 feeds per day

Most people do well with **three to five** eating occasions. Examples:

**Three-feed day (simple)**  
1. Breakfast — protein + carbs  
2. Lunch — protein + carbs + veg  
3. Dinner — protein + carbs + veg  
(+ optional protein snack if the daily total is short)

**Four-feed day (common workday)**  
1. Breakfast  
2. Mid-morning or post-training feed  
3. Lunch  
4. Dinner  

**Five-feed day (high protein / long days)**  
Add a yoghurt, shake, or meat-and-fruit snack between larger meals so you’re not trying to force 180 g of protein into two sittings.

There is no moral prize for intermittent fasting or for six tiny meals. Pick the structure that fits your shifts, digestion, and hunger.

### Default high-protein foods (build your roster)

Rotate what you actually like:

| Category | Easy defaults |
|----------|----------------|
| Meat / poultry | Chicken thigh or breast, lean beef mince, turkey, steak when budget allows |
| Fish | Tinned tuna/salmon, frozen fish fillets |
| Dairy / alternatives | Greek yoghurt, cottage cheese, high-protein milk drinks, cheese in measured amounts |
| Eggs | Whole eggs, egg whites if you need volume |
| Convenience | Rotisserie chicken, pre-cooked chicken, quality deli turkey, protein powder/whey or alternative |
| Plant-forward adds | Tofu, tempeh, lentils/beans alongside animal protein if you like them |

### Carb and plant defaults

- Rice, potatoes, sweet potatoes, oats, bread, wraps, pasta
- Fruit you will actually eat
- Frozen mixed veg, bagged salad, broccoli, carrots — boring on purpose

### Grocery patterns that survive real life

Shop like a systems person:

1. **Protein first** on the list (enough for 3–4 days minimum).
2. **Carb staples** in bulk (rice, oats, potatoes).
3. **Frozen veg** so “no fresh produce” is not an excuse.
4. **Emergency protein** (tins, yoghurt tubs, powder) for the day everything goes wrong.
5. Same breakfast five days a week is a **feature**, not a failure of creativity.

Optional add-ons that save adherence:

- pre-washed salad kits (watch the dressing calories)
- microwave rice pouches for true disaster days
- a dedicated “site snack box” that always lives in the work bag

If you only shop when the fridge is empty and you’re already hungry, you will buy chaos. Shop on a schedule. Hunger is a terrible procurement officer.

### Busy-day / construction / long-shift defaults

When the day starts early and finishes filthy:

- **Cook once, pack twice:** night-before rice + chicken + veg in two containers.
- **Cold options:** Greek yoghurt tubs, fruit, wraps with turkey/chicken, boiled eggs, ready-to-drink protein if it helps.
- **Site-friendly:** foods that don’t need a microwave drama; ice brick in the esky/cooler bag.
- **Post-shift anchor:** a default dinner you can assemble half-asleep (rotisserie + microwave potato + frozen veg + yoghurt).
- **Missed lunch protocol:** do not “start tomorrow.” Hit protein with a shake + whatever solid food you can get; log it; move on.
- **Overtime protocol:** if the day runs long, keep a shelf-stable backup (tuna + crackers, protein bars you actually tolerate, powder + shaker) so the only option is not the servo pie plus regret.

Parents get an extra constraint: kid food in the house. That does not ban biscuits from the planet. It means your defaults are pre-decided so their snacks are not automatically your dinner. Measured shares beat “I’ll just have a bit” amnesia.

### Sample day shapes (illustrative — not prescriptions)

**STRIP-leaning workday (~protein-first)**  
- Breakfast: oats + Greek yoghurt + berries  
- Lunch: chicken, rice, mixed veg  
- Snack: whey shake + apple  
- Dinner: lean mince, potatoes, salad

**Long-shift day**

- Dinner: rotisserie chicken, microwave rice, frozen veg

**LIFT-leaning higher-carb day**  
- Same protein anchors  
- Bigger rice/potato portions around training  
- Extra milk/fruit if appetite is the limiter

Adjust portions to *your* calorie target. The pattern matters more than copying someone else’s plate photo.

## Hunger management in STRIP, social meals, and common mistakes

STRIP hunger is real. Pretending you can white-knuckle misery for forty weeks is how people rebound into chaos.

### Hunger management that actually helps

- **High protein** at every feed.
- **High-volume veg** and fruit for chew and fullness.
- **Potatoes and other satisfying carbs** beat living on sad salad energy.
- **Diet soft drinks / coffee / tea** (within your caffeine-sleep trade-offs) as tools, not personality.
- **Steps** — walk more; it helps expenditure and often mood.
- **Sleep** — short sleep makes hunger louder and adherence dumber.
- **Keep tasty foods in the plan** in measured amounts so you are not building a forbidden-food explosion for Saturday.
- **Protein desserts** (yoghurt + cocoa, high-protein pudding hacks) when sweet cravings show up.

Hunger has flavours. Learn which one you’re dealing with:

| Feeling | Often means | Better move |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Empty + shaky + obsessed | Deficit too deep, carbs too low, or long gap between feeds | Bring a feed forward; raise calories if persistent |
| Bored mouth / “want something” | Habit, stress, salt/crunch craving | High-volume crunchy veg, gum, tea, planned snack |
| Night raiding | Under-ate earlier, poor dinner protein, sleep debt | Bigger daytime protein + solid dinner; protect bedtime |
| Post-alcohol hunger | Disinhibition + poor sleep | Plan drinks; protein-first next morning; no spiral |

If hunger is unbearable and training is falling apart, your deficit is too aggressive or your food selection is too low-volume. Fix the inputs. Suffering is not proof of progress.

### Social meals without blowing the week

- Eat protein-forward; enjoy the occasion; estimate and log.
- Front-load the week with tighter adherence if you know a big dinner is coming — or just accept one higher day inside a solid weekly average.
- Do not “earn” social meals with punishment cardio cosplay that ruins recovery.
- Do not turn one dinner into a four-day off-plan spiral. The spiral is the problem, not the steak.

Family BBQs, work lunches, and kids’ leftovers are part of adult life. The skill is staying in the weekly average without becoming the person who brings a tub of weighed chicken to every event — unless that is genuinely your preference and your social life can handle it. Most people do better with flexible estimation plus a strong home baseline.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it costs you | Fix |
|---------|-------------------|-----|
| Tracking weekdays, “forgetting” weekends | Fake deficit; stalled fat loss | Log Friday–Sunday like they count — because they do |
| Cutting protein to make carbs/fats fit aesthetics | Soft, hungry, weaker | Lock protein first |
| Changing calorie target every three days | Noise, no signal | Run 2–4 weeks |
| Oil/peanut butter “eyeballing” | Hundreds of hidden kcal | Weigh the dense stuff |
| Dirty bulk as month-one identity | Extra fat, then a miserable cut | Earn LIFT; use a modest surplus |
| Zero fun foods → binge cycle | Adherence collapse | Budget foods you like |
| Scale-only judgement | Bad phase flips | Photos + waist + strength |
| App-hopping and plan-hopping | Zero skill built | One logger, one phase |
| Cardio punishment to erase logging gaps | Recovery debt | Budget honestly instead |
| Copying someone else’s macros | Wrong size, wrong job, wrong phase | Set from *your* weight and STRIPLINE phase |

## Label-reading walkthrough (AU supermarket)

1. Check **serving size** vs what you actually pour.  
2. Protein per 100 g — compare lean meats/yoghurts.  
3. Energy (kJ): divide by 4.184 for kcal approx if needed.  
4. “No added sugar” ≠ low calorie.  
5. Sauces: log a tablespoon weighed once, reuse that custom food.  
6. Barcode scan → verify protein and calories aren’t fantasy entries.
