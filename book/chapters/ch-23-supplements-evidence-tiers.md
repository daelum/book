# Chapter 23 — Supplements: What Is Worth It

Let’s be honest: the supplement industry is world-class at selling hope in a tub. Some products earn their shelf space. Most are expensive pee with good branding. This chapter is a sorting hat — not a shopping spree.

**Understanding ≠ “you must buy this.”** Food, training, sleep, and protein still beat almost every powder.

## 1. WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

### How we’ll talk about evidence

| Class | Meaning in this book |
|-------|----------------------|
| **A–B** | Strong / moderate research support for a clear effect |
| **C** | Emerging or limited |
| **D** | Expert/practical consensus without airtight trials |
| **E** | Anecdote |
| **F** | Weak, misleading, or marketing cosplay |

Claims here stay practical. Exact paper lists belong in the citation index later — we won’t invent DOIs in the meantime.

### Tier 1 — usually worth considering (when basics exist)

| Supplement | Why people use it | Notes |
|------------|-------------------|-------|
| **Creatine monohydrate** | Strength, power, lean-mass support in training | Best-supported performance supplement in healthy users at studied intakes; daily use beats magical timing theatre. Loading optional — saturation can take longer without it. Prefer plain monohydrate. *(Claim `clm_35b78a69d71c`; bib `bib_06b671ef36`, `bib_0ad83801ce`.)* |
| **Protein powder** | Convenience toward protein target | Food can meet needs; powder is a busy-day tool, not superior magic when totals match. *(Claim `clm_808a8b7a439d`.)* |
| **Caffeine** | Focus, training performance | Can aid performance for many people; dose carefully and **don’t wreck sleep**. Anxiety/tolerance are individual. *(Claim `clm_b8f879e58769`; bib `bib_deb598f2ee`.)* |

### Tier 1.5 — health insurance *if* diet/sun is poor (not physique cheat codes)

| Supplement | Notes |
|------------|-------|
| **Vitamin D** | Deficiency-conditional — bloodwork > guessing; soft Tier-1 physique ROI until personalised. |
| **Fish oil / EPA+DHA** | Useful if fatty fish is rare; optional if diet already covers it. Soft cite status. |

### Tier 2 — situational

| Supplement | When it might matter |
|------------|----------------------|
| **Electrolytes** | Heavy sweat, low-carb phases, cramping contexts |
| **Fibre supplements** | If diet is sparse and digestion suffers |
| **Beta-alanine** | High-rep burning sets — paresthesia is normal; benefit is niche |
| **Citrulline** | Pump/performance interest — nice-to-have, not foundational |
| **Multivitamin** | Insurance for chaotic eaters — not a physique cheat code |

### Tier 3 — usually skip for Year 1 physique ROI

Fat burners with fairy dust blends. Testosterone “boosters” that don’t move the needle. Most proprietary pre-workouts that are caffeine + marketing. Anything promising stage looks in two weeks.

If a label needs seventeen unverifiable compounds to sound scientific, your wallet is the experiment.

### What supplements cannot do

- Replace progressive overload  
- Fix a 1,000-calorie weekend black hole  
- Spot-reduce belly fat  
- Turn Pathway C into a billboard without the boring work  

Vegan or restricted diets may need attention to B12, creatine, iron, etc. — that’s nutrition care, not influencer stacks.

## 2. WHY IT MATTERS

Beginners love stacks because stacks feel like progress without another hard set. Experienced trainees buy fewer things and train harder. Get the hierarchy right and you’ll stop funding someone else’s boat.

## 3. WHAT EXPERIENCED BODYBUILDERS DO

- Creatine + protein convenience + caffeine (if tolerated) covers most of the useful list.  
- They check labels for actual creatine monohydrate — not mystery “matrix” blends.  
- They don’t expect supplements to outrun sleep debt.  
- They treat bloodwork as smarter than guesswork for deficiencies.  
- They laugh at the bloke with £200/month in pills and no squat pattern.

## 4. YOUR ACTION PLAN

1. **Hit food protein and calorie bias first** for 4 weeks before adding anything beyond maybe creatine.  
2. **If you add one thing:** creatine monohydrate daily (follow label serving; this is not medical advice).  
3. **Use whey/casein/plant powder only** to fill gaps.  
4. **Audit caffeine** so afternoon doses aren’t nuking sleep.  
5. **Skip fat-burner aisle** in Year 1.  
6. **Talk to a clinician** before correcting suspected deficiencies with high-dose vitamins.
