### Richer first-week plan (hour by hour isn’t required — decisions are)

**Day 0 (today, even if it’s ugly):**  
Photos. Waist. Date. Folder named with the date so future-you can’t “lose” baselines. Decision written: STRIP / recomp / documented exception. Food Bro PDF downloaded. Calories + protein copied into the same note as the programme.

**Day 1:**  
Session one as written — even if short. Log every working set. Hit protein without negotiating a new diet identity. Message your LINK: “Started. Targets are X calories / Y protein. Check-in day is ___.”

**Day 2:**  
Steps floor. Sleep floor. Prep one default meal that makes protein easy tomorrow. No programme edits.

**Day 3:**  
Session two or rest as written. If hunger is loud, that’s data inside STRIP — not a reason to bulk. Keep the weekly average in mind.

**Day 4–5:**  
Stay on LINE. Social event? Plan it inside the week; don’t burn the template. If you miss a session, run MED — don’t declare a restart.

**Day 6–7:**  
Second (or third) session as scheduled. Brief adherence note: sessions done, protein days hit, steps average, one sentence on energy. Send it. Book next week’s check-in. Do **not** change the programme because motivation felt mid.

That’s a first week. Unromantic. Sufficient.

### First-week traps to refuse

- Rewriting the split after session one because a reel disagreed.  
- Cutting another 500 kcal because the scale didn’t move in 72 hours.  
- Buying five supplements before you’ve hit protein four days.  
- Skipping photos because you “don’t look ready.” Baselines are supposed to be honest, not flattering.  
- Waiting for your LINK to be perfect coaching — a ruthless mate counts until the formal door opens.

STRIPLINE doesn’t require perfect conditions. It requires sequence and reps.

- **Physique cost:** another twelve months of soft-middle photos and wardrobe negotiation.

## FAQ

**Do I have to get stage-lean before I build any muscle?**  
No. You lift during STRIP. Fat-loss-first means fat is the priority bias — not “never stimulate muscle.”

**I’m skinny. Shouldn’t I bulk?**  
Only if photos show you’re already lean. Soft middle + “I’m a hardgainer” is usually skinny-fat cosplay. Document exceptions; don’t invent them.

**How aggressive should the deficit be?**  
Moderate enough to train and adhere. Start ~300–500 kcal below maintenance for many people; adjust from trend data.

**Can I recomp instead of cut?**  
Often yes early — especially skinny-fat — if lifting and protein are excellent and the middle still improves. Recomp isn’t a forever fog to avoid decisions.

**What if I miss a week?**  
Run MED. Resume. Don’t burn the programme and start a new identity.

**How much protein?**  
~1.6–2.2 g/kg as a practical band. Use Food Bro + book hierarchy. Consistency beats precision theatre.

**Is cardio required?**  
Steps help a lot in STRIP. Extra cardio is optional if it doesn’t trash lifting or recovery.

**When do I move to LIFT emphasis?**  
When midsection trajectory and adherence criteria you wrote are met — not when boredom hits.

**Do I need supplements?**  
Optional. Creatine, caffeine, protein powder are the sensible tier once basics exist.

**What about PEDs / peptides / GLP-1s?**  
Read the appendix for literacy. No protocols in this book. Fundamentals first. Discussion of personal fit: **[PLACEHOLDER: paid offer / coach funnel — pending agreement]**.

**How do I track without obsessing?**  
Photos + waist + strength + weekly averages. Simplify if the app runs your mood.

**What if my job destroys my schedule?**  
Shorter sessions, fewer days, protein defaults, step floor, LINK still active. Busy is normal here — chaos programmes aren’t.

**Can women run STRIPLINE?**  
Yes — same sequence principles; individual calorie/protein set-up still personal. This book doesn’t pretend one number fits every body.

**How long until I see change?**  
Waist/photos often shift in weeks if adherence is real; “best physique of your life” is a twelve-month project, not a weekend.

**What if I don’t have a coach yet?**  
Use a ruthless mate as LINK. Keep the placeholder for formal coaching when available.

**Should I weigh every day?**  
Daily weighing is fine if you use weekly averages and don’t let single mornings run your emotions. If the scale runs your mood, weigh less often and lean harder on photos, waist, and strength.

**What is MED again?**  
Minimum effective dose for a bad week: protein floor, step floor, abbreviated sessions that keep the pattern alive. MED is a bridge, not a new lifestyle. Resume the full LINE when the week allows.

**Can I drink alcohol and still STRIP?**  
Sometimes, inside a weekly average — with honesty about calories and recovery. Chronic weekend black holes aren’t a mystery; they’re a plan leak. Adjust the plan or the drinking pattern; don’t pretend both are unlimited.

**I have kids and no quiet evenings. Still viable?**  
Yes. Shorter sessions, earlier training if possible, protein defaults that don’t need chef energy, LINK that texts not essays. The method assumes real life; it doesn’t require a monastery.

**How do I pick lifts if I’m a beginner?**  
Simple patterns you can load safely: squat or leg press, hinge (RDL or machine), push, pull, a carry or core finisher. Progression rule written. Ego maxing left at the door. Stage 5’s playbook is the deeper map — LINE still starts with what you’ll actually show up for.

**What if my waist isn’t moving after a month?**  
Check adherence first (food average, steps, weekends). Then one lever: small calorie drop or step bump. Don’t change programme, diet religion, and supplement stack in the same weekend.

**What if strength is crashing in STRIP?**  
Protein, sleep, and deficit size first. Panic cuts trash performance. Deload if needed. Keep lifting — don’t become a cardio-only tourist.

**Do I need a fancy watch?**  
No. Steps help; a phone pedometer or honest daily walk total is enough to start. Tools amplify; they don’t replace LINE.

**How many times can I fail and still be “on” STRIPLINE?**  
As many bad weeks as life throws — if you resume. Failure is quitting the sequence. Misses are weather.

**Is twelve months mandatory before any pride?**  
No. Take interim wins. Just don’t confuse a four-week waist change with the full project. The book is named for the year because the year is what builds a physique you keep.

**What if I already lift hard but look soft?**  
That’s often STRIP + LINE nutrition honesty, not a new advanced split. Hard training on a soft surplus still yields a soft photo.

**Can I run STRIPLINE with home kit only?**  
Yes if you can overload over time — adjustable dumbbells, bands plus load progression, or progressive push-up/row variations with honest difficulty increases. If you can’t progress, join a gym or add loadable kit. LIFT requires progressive stimulus.

**What if I’m already on a cut that feels miserable?**  
Misery that trashes training and protein isn’t STRIP — it’s a crash. Raise food toward a sustainable deficit, keep lifting, reassess in two weeks. Adherence is part of the physics.

**How do I handle holidays inside STRIPLINE?**  
Decide before the trip: maintenance week, lighter deficit, or planned surplus days inside a monthly average. Keep LINE’s training where possible. Don’t invent a new annual identity in December and call it strategy.

**Should I tell people I’m doing this?**  
Tell your LINK for sure. Everyone else is optional. Public announcements without a check-in human are usually theatre.

**What if photos demotivate me?**  
Good — baselines aren’t supposed to flatter. File them. Compare only under the same conditions at review dates. Daily mirror audits in bad lighting are not a scoreboard.

**Can I change my Food Bro numbers mid-block?**  
If body weight or phase changes enough that the targets are clearly wrong — yes, at a review. Not because Thursday felt hungry.

**Is walking enough “cardio”?**  
For many people in STRIP, a step floor is the main movement lever beside lifting. Add structured cardio only if it helps adherence or conditioning without wrecking recovery.

**What if my LINK flakes?**  
Replace them. The role matters more than the original person. Silence returns you to negotiating with yourself.

**Do I need to read the appendix before I start?**  
No. Start STRIPLINE. Read advanced-tools literacy when curiosity or Year 2 decisions show up — not as a delay tactic.

**How do I know the first week “worked”?**  
You took baselines, locked numbers, trained, ate protein, and sent a check-in. That’s success for week one. Waist magic is not the grading rubric yet.

## First-month bridge (after week one)

Weeks 2–4 are where novelty dies and LINE earns its name.

- Keep the same programme.  
- Hit protein most days.  
- Defend steps and sleep floors.  
- Send weekly LINK notes even when they’re boring.  
- At week 4: photos, waist, strength glance, adherence score. One lever maximum if stalled.  
- Refuse: new split, new diet religion, new supplement identity.

If week two feels flat, congratulations — you’re in the real programme. Flat is where wrong order usually hops. STRIPLINE stays.

### FAQ add-ons for edge cases

**I travel weekly for work.**  
Lock a hotel-capable MED session (push/pull/hinge/squat pattern with whatever kit exists) plus protein defaults you can buy at a petrol station without turning it into a joke. Full LINE at home; MED on the road; averages across the week.

**I train with a mate who hops programmes.**  
Your LINE is yours. Train together if it helps LINK; don’t outsource your template to their boredom.

**I’ve been injured.**  
Get clearance and diagnosis. LINE becomes the pattern you can load pain-free. STRIPLINE doesn’t require heroics on a damaged joint; it requires honesty and progressive work inside what’s allowed.

**I’m impatient and angry this is “only” twelve months.**  
Good. Use the impatience to refuse hopping. Twelve focused months beats five angry years of restarts.

**What if I hate tracking food?**  
Use Food Bro targets as portions and defaults; tighten tracking only if waist won’t move. Some people need grams; some need consistent plates. Results decide, ego doesn’t.

**Can I do two-a-days?**  
Usually unnecessary for this book’s reader. Earn recovery first. More sessions aren’t LINE — finishing the ones you wrote is.

**What if my partner cooks differently to the plan?**  
Defaults and communication beat secret diets. Hit protein with add-ons if needed; keep the weekly average adult. LINE isn’t a hostage situation — it’s shared clarity where possible.

**How strict is “lock” on LINE?**  
Locked means you don’t hop philosophies mid-block. Substitutions for pain, travel, or equipment are allowed when they keep the pattern and progression rule alive. Locked is not brittle; brittle plans shatter on contact with Tuesday.

**Should I deload on a schedule or when needed?**  
Pick a rule and write it — e.g. every 6–8 weeks, or when performance and joints complain together. Random deloads that are actually skipped weeks without a plan don’t count as recovery strategy.

**What goes in the Year 2 sketch?**  
Another STRIP polish, a growth block, skill goals, and whether advanced-tools literacy conversations belong under supervision. Sketch beats cliff. Details wait until months 10–12 when you have evidence.

### Checklist commentary (why each box earns its ink)

**Fat-loss-first / exception documented** — Stops the bulk-into-soft-photo reflex. Writing the exception prevents cosplay.

**One written programme** — Ends hopping. If it isn’t written, it isn’t locked.

**Protein most days** — Protects muscle in STRIP and feeds LIFT. “Most days” is adult language for consistency without perfection theatre.

**Progressive overload logged** — Memory lies; notebooks don’t. No log, no proof of LIFT.

**Steps + sleep floors** — Quiet levers that save aggressive calorie cuts and trashed recovery.

**Human accountability** — The plan outside your mood. Mate or coach; silence is how weeks evaporate.

**Review, one lever** — Diagnostics without identity crisis. Multiple levers at once teach you nothing.

**Food Bro PDF filed** — Numbers stop being vibes. Revisit when body weight or phase changes enough to matter.

**Bad-week MED** — Guarantees you have a bridge before life asks for one.

**Year sketch by months 10–12** — Prevents cliff-edge panic and random Year 2 chaos.

Tick boxes in ink. Unticked boxes are where years hide.

Print the checklist or copy it into your notes app. Revisit it every review. If three boxes go grey for a month, you don’t need a new book — you need LINK to ask why, and LINE to resume. The checklist is not decoration; it’s the short form of *The 12-Month Body*.

### One more FAQ block for nerves

**What if I’m embarrassed to send photos to my LINK?**

**What if I start and stop again?**

**Is this compatible with shift work / FIFO / overtime seasons?**  
Yes with MED and flexible day placement. The plan moves on the calendar; the targets and progression rule don’t reinvent themselves every swing.
