# Stage 4 — Why Listen to Me

I’m not writing this as a famous bodybuilder with a secret formula. I’m writing it as someone whose life — and physique — turned around in twelve months when the order finally got honest.

Here’s the real story. I’m not inventing extras.

## 2026: fat loss that wasn’t the physique yet

In **2026** I lost **over 25 kg**. On paper that sounds like the win. In the mirror it was more complicated. I went from essentially carrying a lot of fat to looking **skinny-fat** — lighter, softer in the wrong places, not the athletic shape I actually wanted.

Then I **put weight back on**.

That part matters. A lot of transformation content skips the ugly middle: the rebound, the confusion, the moment you realise the scale lied about the goal. Lower weight isn’t automatically a better body. I learned that the hard way.

### What skinny-fat after a big loss actually teaches

You can do something “right” on the scale and still miss the point. Clothes get looser. Relatives congratulate you. Inside the mirror, the midsection and the lack of muscle structure still argue. If you’ve been there, you know the weird grief of succeeding at subtraction and failing at composition.

I don’t need to dress that up with invented drama. The fact pattern is enough: big fat loss → skinny-fat look → regain. That’s the wrong-order / incomplete-order lesson lived, not lectured.

## The coach and the system

I got an **experienced coach**. I learned **the system** — not a vibe, not a random stack of tips, a way of running training and food with someone keeping me accountable.

After that, I lost **over another 15 kg** while **putting on muscle at the same time**. Different game. Composition, not just subtraction.

Now I feel **bigger and stronger at around 75 kg** than I did at around **103 kg**. More toned. More like the project was about building something, not only deleting something.

That’s the whole proof I’m offering you: same human, different order, different outcome — fat-loss-first when it needed to be, a locked plan, progressive training, and accountability that didn’t let me quietly rewrite the rules every Thursday.

### ~75 versus ~103 — the feel that matters

The numbers are landmarks, not a prescription for your body. What matters is the contradiction people don’t expect until they live it: you can weigh less and feel more solid, move better, and look more like the goal — when muscle and fat finally move in the right directions together under a system.

~103 kg with a lot of fat is one kind of “big.” ~75 kg with more muscle and less fat is a different kind of capable. Same person. Different composition. That’s why this book refuses to worship the scale as the whole courtroom.

## Why systems clicked for me — under real life, not unlimited gym time

I’m a **father of two young girls**. During this turnaround I was also working **~70-hour weeks in construction** — on top of a brain that also lives in **software**, data, plans, and systems. This did **not** happen because I had a content-creator schedule, a private chef, and three free hours of “optimal training” every day.

It happened because the plan had to survive fatherhood and brutal hours: locked sessions that fit the calendar, protein defaults that didn’t need a perfect kitchen every night, and a coach keeping me accountable when Thursday-me wanted to negotiate.

If you’re reading this after a twelve-hour day with kids who still need you, you’re the audience. STRIPLINE assumes constraints. Unlimited recovery is not the prerequisite.

My background in **construction** and **software development** meant I already trusted **data, plans, and following systems**. When training finally got the same respect as a build schedule or a codebase — written plan, tracked inputs, review loops — results stopped being mystical.

If you’re the type who can follow a procedure at work but treats the gym like improvisational theatre, you’re not broken. You’re just running the wrong process in the place that needs process most.

### Construction brain meets training

On a site, you don’t “feel your way” through a structural sequence and hope the inspection passes. You follow an order. You check what’s done. You don’t skip foundations because the finishings look more exciting on Instagram. Seventy-hour weeks don’t leave room for programme-hopping cosplay — you run the one plan you locked.

### Software brain meets food and lifting

In code, vibes without version control become chaos. Training without a log, nutrition without targets, and weeks without review are the same bug with sweat on them. Once I treated adherence like something you can see — sessions done, protein hit, photos on schedule — the fog lifted. Not because I’m special. Because systems are what I already trusted everywhere else — and because accountability meant someone noticed when the log went quiet after a monster week on the tools.

I’m not saying anybody gets the same photos in the same weeks. Construction days and software sprints didn’t make me exempt from hunger or missed sessions. They made me hungry for a process that survives real weeks.

If you need a guru myth, this isn’t your book. If you need a sequence and a reason to trust twelve months of boring continuity, you’re in the right place.
