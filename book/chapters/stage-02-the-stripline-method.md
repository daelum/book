# Stage 2 — The STRIPLINE Method

In twelve months you’ll change how you look by stripping fat first, locking one plan, and building under accountability — without bulk-before-you’re-lean chaos or another abandoned programme PDF.

That sentence is the whole product. Everything else is implementation — weeks, reviews, and the refusal to hop when boredom asks politely.

**The STRIPLINE Method** has four moves: **STRIP → LINE → LIFT → LINK**. They aren’t motivational posters. They’re the order of operations for *The 12-Month Body* — four moves, one spine, no competing brand.

Old gym roadmaps still talk about Foundation, Build, Refine, Peak. Those can still describe *months inside* the year. They are not distinctive enough as a method, and they often smuggle in “pick your calorie religion” before they’ve told you to get the midsection honest. STRIPLINE fixes the spine: fat-loss-first by default, one locked plan, muscle emphasis when the base deserves it, accountability so you don’t negotiate the plan into dust.

## STRIP — fat down before you obsess over getting bigger

**STRIP** means your first job is getting body fat moving in the right direction until the photo stops lying.

Not “get stage lean in six weeks.” Not punishment cardio until you hate training. A sustainable deficit (or a recomp that still prioritises midsection progress) while you lift hard and eat enough protein that you don’t evaporate.

### What STRIP looks like in practice

- Calories set for fat loss (or honest recomp) — weekly average matters more than one perfect day.
- Protein high enough to protect muscle (~1.6–2.2 g per kg as a practical band; use goal weight if body weight is very high).
- Progressive resistance training kept non-negotiable — you’re not “just dieting.”
- Steps / daily movement as a quiet fat-loss helper.
- Photos + waist + strength as the scoreboard; scale as supporting actor.

STRIP is where most people should spend early months even if their ego wants a bulk. Soft middles don’t become athletic by eating in a surplus and hoping shoulders outrun the belly.

### A week in the life of STRIP (ordinary adult edition)

Monday: train upper or full body as written. Hit protein. Steps on the watch. Sleep defended even if the day ran long.

Tuesday: maybe legs or a second session. Hunger shows up mid-afternoon — you don’t invent a new diet; you eat the plan’s meal or a default high-protein option you already decided exists.

Wednesday: rest or active recovery. Steps still count. No “I earned a free-for-all because I trained hard Monday.”

Thursday: train. Log loads. Notice one lift moved a rep — that’s the quiet win STRIP protects.

Friday: social meal in the diary. You don’t cancel the week; you bank calories earlier or accept a weekly average that still trends down.

Weekend: kids, overtime, life. Bad-week minimum ready: protein floor, two short sessions if needed, step floor, no programme rewrite.

That’s STRIP. Not aesthetic suffering. Direction with a life attached.

### STRIP examples by starting point

**Higher body fat:** obvious. Modest deficit, high protein, lift like muscle matters, steps up. Expect clothes and waist to talk before the mirror becomes flattering under bad lighting.

**Skinny-fat:** often a lighter deficit or recomp — still midsection-first. Don’t dirty bulk because you feel “small.” Small and soft is not the same problem as lean and underfed.

**Restarter:** you’ve lifted before. STRIP still wins if the photo is soft. Experience doesn’t exempt you from physics; it just means LINE can reuse skills faster once you stop hopping.

### The lean-hardgainer exception (documented — not the default)

If you are already lean, light-framed, and genuinely struggle to eat enough — photos show little fat to strip — your STRIP phase may be short or mostly “stop cutting yourself into nothing.” You still LINE (lock a plan). Your LIFT may arrive sooner with a small surplus. Write the exception down. Don’t use “I’m a hardgainer” as cosplay while carrying a soft midsection.

**How to document the exception properly**

1. Same-condition photos: front, side, back. Good light optional; consistency mandatory.  
2. Waist measure. If the midsection is clearly soft, you do not get the exception.  
3. One-sentence rationale: “Already lean; underfed history; priority is progressive surplus under a locked plan.”  
4. Review date in 4–6 weeks. If waist climbs faster than strength and limb fullness, you lied to yourself — return to STRIP without drama.

True hardgainers often undereat relative to training, fear “getting fat,” and live in a cut mentality that keeps them small forever. The exception is permission to feed the work — not permission to eat chaos. LINE still locks calories upward with intent. LINK still asks whether you hit them.

If you’re unsure, default to STRIP/recomp until the photo votes. Ego prefers the bulk story. Honesty prefers evidence.

## LINE — lock one plan and stop hopping

**LINE** means you draw the line: this is the programme, these are the food targets, these are the sleep/step floors, these are the review dates. Then you run it.

A programme is not a religion. It’s a way to organise hard sets across the week so muscles get trained often enough, recovered enough, and progressed enough around your actual calendar. One boring template beaten for twelve weeks beats three clever templates abandoned at week three.

### What gets locked

1. **Training template** — days, lifts, progression rule (e.g. double progression: add reps in a range, then add load).
2. **Nutrition targets** — calories + protein at minimum; carbs/fats fill the rest in a way you can digest, train on, and repeat.
3. **Recovery floors** — sleep minimum you’ll defend; deload rule.
4. **Movement floor** — steps especially in STRIP.
5. **Review ritual** — every 2–4 weeks: photos, waist, strength, adherence. Change **one** lever if stalled.

LINE is also where you stop collecting rules. Meal timing, fancy supplements, advanced intensity techniques — seasoning. Calories, protein, progressive overload, sleep — the meal.

If a diet style tanks your adherence every time you try it, stop collecting rematches with the same fad. Adherence is the cost centre that decides whether any approach works.

### A week in the life of LINE

Sunday night (ten minutes): glance at the written week. No redesign. If travel exists, swap days — don’t invent a new philosophy.

Training days: same lifts in the same order unless pain forces a substitution you already listed as an alternate. Logbook open before the first warm-up set.

Food: defaults ready — breakfast protein, work lunch that hits the number, dinner that doesn’t require chef theatre. Tracking if you need it; averages if daily weighing makes you mental.

Review Friday or every second Sunday: adherence score honest. Missed two sessions? That’s data. Not a personality verdict. One lever only if stalled: calories, steps, or a progression tweak — not all three plus a new split.

### LINE examples

**Shift worker:** lock a 3-day full-body rotating on available days, not “Mon/Wed/Fri” fantasy. Protein targets stay; timing flexes.

**Parent evenings:** shorter sessions with the big lifts first. Optional accessories only if time remains. LINE protects the core, not the Instagram accessory list.

**Office + gym after work:** commute and hunger are the enemies. Pre-packed protein and a fixed start time beat “I’ll go if I feel like it.”

## LIFT — build muscle on a cleaner base

**LIFT** is progressive overload with intent: more load, more reps, better control, or more quality hard sets over time — not random sweating.

You train during STRIP too. LIFT as a *phase emphasis* means: once the midsection trajectory is honest (or your documented exception applies), you bias more of the year toward muscle gain — food up toward maintenance or a small surplus, volume progression where recovery allows, weak-point work without novelty addiction.

### What LIFT is not

- Ego maxing every session because you feel motivated.
- Copying advanced volume from someone who recovers like a professional athlete with a nap schedule.
- Abandoning protein and sleep because “I’m bulking now.”

Muscle grows when you give it a reason, the raw materials, and enough recovery to finish the job. Training is the request. Recovery is the construction. Food decides whether the request is easy, hard, or ignored.

### A week in the life of LIFT emphasis

Calories sit near maintenance or a small surplus you chose on purpose. Hunger is less dramatic; the job is hitting protein and not turning every weekend into an untracked surplus avalanche.

Sessions push progression: add a rep, add a kilo, improve the pause, clean up the last set. Weak points get one or two honest movements — not a new programme identity.

Sleep and deloads matter more because volume may creep. LINK asks whether you’re actually recovering or just eating more and calling it a bulk.

### LIFT examples

**After a successful STRIP:** waist down, strength mostly held, photos cleaner — raise food, keep the same template, chase overload for 8–12 weeks.

**Hardgainer exception:** small surplus from week one of the locked plan; patience measured in months of logged progression, not fortnightly scale panic.

**Still soft at month six:** you don’t “LIFT harder” by force. You keep stripping or recomping. LIFT emphasis is earned by criteria, not calendar boredom.

## LINK — accountability so the plan survives you

**LINK** is the human loop. Someone checks. You report. The plan stays outside your mood.

### What LINK looks like weekly

- You send: sessions done, protein hit?, steps, one sentence on hunger/energy.
- They ask: did you follow the written plan — not the rewritten Thursday version?
- You review photos on schedule, not when the lighting is flattering.

LINK also covers Year 2 decisions later: stay in a growth block, another STRIP polish, advanced-tools literacy with supervision — not 1 a.m. impulse after a reel.

### A week in the life of LINK

Check-in is boring on purpose. “3/3 sessions. Protein 6/7. Steps average 8k. Sleep rough Wednesday.” Your person replies with one question that hurts a little: “What happens Thursday when you’re tired?” You answer with the MED plan, not a speech.

Monthly photo drop isn’t a performance. Same bathroom, same light if you can, same poses. Comparison to month one beats comparison to strangers.

Without LINK, LINE slowly becomes a suggestion. With LINK, missed weeks get recovered instead of mythologised.

## How STRIPLINE maps onto twelve months

Think in phases, not vibes.

| Months | STRIPLINE bias | What “good” looks like |
|--------|----------------|-------------------------|
| 1–3 | **STRIP + LINE** | Fat moving or midsection improving; plan locked; lifts logged; habits installed |
| 4–6 | **STRIP continues or transition** | Still honest about fat; strength mostly protected; adherence high |
| 7–9 | **LIFT emphasis when criteria met** | If midsection allows, push muscle; if not, keep stripping without drama |
| 10–12 | **LIFT + LINK** | Photos vs month 1; strength PRs; Year 2 sketch; accountability still on |

Inside those months you can still use words like foundation (skill), build (stress), refine (aim), peak (show the work). They are subtitles under STRIPLINE — not a competing brand.

### Month mapping examples (stories, not prescriptions)

**Example A — soft start:** Months 1–6 mostly STRIP + LINE. Waist drops. Strength holds. Months 7–10 LIFT emphasis. Months 11–12 polish, photos, Year 2 plan with LINK still weekly.

**Example B — skinny-fat:** Months 1–4 recomp/STRIP bias with aggressive protein and lifting. Middle tightens, limbs thicken slightly. Months 5–12 gradual LIFT if criteria met — or continued recomp if the photo still softens under surplus.

**Example C — lean hardgainer exception:** Month 1 short STRIP-as-“stop undereating,” LINE hard. Months 2–12 LIFT with patient surplus, same template, LINK watching waist so surplus doesn’t become denial.

**Example D — restarter with history:** Month 1 audit: delete novelty, lock one template, decide STRIP vs LIFT from photos. Then follow the table without reinventing your identity every block.

Your calendar will differ. The spine shouldn’t.

## Starting-point modifiers (not alternate religions)

People start different. The method doesn’t.

- **Higher body fat:** STRIP is obviously primary. Sustainable deficit, high protein, lift like muscle matters.
- **Skinny-fat:** STRIP/recomp until the middle tightens and limbs thicken — don’t dirty bulk “for aesthetics later.”
- **Already lean hardgainer:** documented exception; LINE hard; LIFT sooner with patient surplus.
- **Restarter with gym history:** LINE means audit and simplify before novelty; then apply STRIP or LIFT based on photos, not ego.

Secondary curiosity about advanced tools never hijacks months 1–3. Fundamentals first. Literacy later.

## Putting the four letters on one page

If you forget everything else from this stage, keep this:

| Move | Job | Failure mode |
|------|-----|--------------|
| **STRIP** | Make the midsection honest | Dirty bulk or crash cut |
| **LINE** | Lock plan + floors + reviews | Programme-hopping |
| **LIFT** | Progressive muscle emphasis when earned | Ego volume / surplus denial |
| **LINK** | Human loop that survives mood | Lone-wolf rewrite culture |

STRIP without LINE is a temporary diet. LINE without LINK slowly dissolves. LIFT before STRIP is the wrong-order classic. LINK without a written line is just someone listening to your vibes. The method is the combination in order.

### How the year should *feel* (not just look)

Months 1–3 often feel annoying: hunger management, learning the template, fewer dopamine hits from novelty. That’s installation, not failure.

Months 4–6 feel quieter if you’re doing it right — less drama, more logged work. People quit here because quiet doesn’t feel like content. Stay.

Months 7–9 either open into LIFT emphasis (food up, chase tissue) or continue honest STRIP without calling yourself broken. Criteria decide, not ego.

Months 10–12 should feel like evidence: photos vs month one, strength story, a Year 2 sketch that isn’t panic. LINK is still on. The year ends with a system, not a cliff.

## What you stop doing when STRIPLINE is real

- Bulking into a soft photo because a podcast said so.
- Changing programmes when motivation is bored.
- Using the scale as a courtroom.
- Quitting protein when appetite is weird.
- Running unsupervised chaos because someone online “looks insane.”
- Treating accountability as optional personality.
- Collecting diet identities instead of weekly averages.
- Confusing research nights with training nights.
- Waiting for perfect sleep before you earn average sleep.
- Using “hardgainer” as a costume for a soft middle.

## Common objections (answered briefly)

**“Won’t I lose muscle if I strip first?”**  
Not if protein is high, lifting stays progressive, and the deficit is adult-sized. Panic cuts lose tissue. STRIP isn’t a panic cut.

**“I only have three days.”**  
LINE can be three days. Frequency fantasies don’t beat finished weeks.

**“I travel constantly.”**  
Defaults travel better than perfect kitchens. Protein targets and a shortened session list beat “I’ll restart when I’m home.”

**“Accountability feels soft.”**  
It’s structural. Planes have checklists. Builds have inspections. Your physique can have a check-in without becoming a personality cult.

**“What if I stall?”**  
Review ritual. One lever. Photos and waist before panic. Stalls are information when LINE is intact; they’re identity crises when you’ve hopped too much to know what you were testing.

**“Can I do STRIPLINE in a commercial gym with limited kit?”**  
Yes. LINE picks patterns you can load: squat/hinge/push/pull/carry variants that exist on your floor. Fancy machines are optional. Progressive hard sets are not.

### Micro-example: locking LINE in one sitting

Open a note. Write:

- Days: e.g. Mon / Wed / Fri  
- Session skeleton: squat pattern, hinge, horizontal push, horizontal pull, vertical push or pull, one arm isolation, one carry or core  
- Progression: 3×6–10; add weight when all sets hit 10 clean  
- Calories / protein: from Food Bro PDF or equivalent — numbers on the page  
- Steps floor / sleep floor  
- LINK human + check-in day  
- Review every 14 or 28 days  

That’s enough to start. Stage 5 elaborates. LINE’s job here is to end the fog, not to win a programming contest.

### Micro-example: STRIP review that doesn’t spiral

Week 4 photos look similar. Waist down 1–2 cm. Strength mostly flat. Adherence 80%. You do **not** rewrite the programme, add a miracle supplement, and slash calories by a thousand. You nudge steps or tighten weekend averages — one lever — and run another block. That’s STRIPLINE maturity: boring corrections beat dramatic reinvention.

### Micro-example: when LIFT waits

Month 6. Strength is up. Midsection still soft in side photos. Boredom says bulk. Criteria say keep stripping or recomp. You keep LINE, keep LINK, delay LIFT emphasis. Ego hates it. The year loves it. Wrong order starts the surplus here; STRIPLINE doesn’t.

## Hardgainer exception — worked detail

Let’s labour this because misuse is common.

**You might be a candidate if:** visible fat is low in honest photos; waist is already tight; training history shows food fear or chronic under-eating; strength rises when calories rise; “cutting” mostly makes you smaller and colder without revealing much muscle.

**You are probably not a candidate if:** side photos show a soft middle; belts tell on you; you rebound between “I’m skinny” feelings and hoodie softness; you want a bulk mainly because surplus is more fun than adherence.

**What the exception changes:** STRIP becomes brief — stop the accidental cut, install LINE, move toward a small surplus sooner.

**What the exception does not change:** you still lock a programme; you still log progressive overload; you still review waist so surplus doesn’t become denial; you still LINK so “I ate big” isn’t an unverified vibe.

**Patient surplus literacy:** small and steady beats dirty chaos. If waist races ahead of strength and limb fullness, you didn’t discover hardgainer destiny — you discovered unstructured overeating. Pull back toward maintenance, keep lifting, reassess. The exception is a lane, not a personality.

**Month sketch for a documented exception:** Month 1 — photos, LINE lock, food up from undereating toward a planned surplus, LINK weekly. Months 2–6 — same template, chase overload, waist watched every review. Months 7–12 — continue LIFT or insert a short STRIP polish if softness crept in. No programme-hopping as celebration.

## STRIP / LINE / LIFT / LINK — failure modes worth naming

**STRIP failure:** crash for two weeks, binge for two, call it a metabolism problem.  
**LINE failure:** perfect spreadsheet, zero weeks finished.  
**LIFT failure:** surplus without progressive stimulus — “dreamer bulk.”  
**LINK failure:** check-ins that are essays about feelings and silent about sessions and protein.

Name the failure early and you can correct without burning the year.

## Another week-in-the-life: mixed reality (STRIP + LINE under pressure)

Your roster looks fine on paper. Then a late job lands, a kid gets sick, and sleep fragments. Wrong order uses that week to scrap the identity. STRIPLINE uses MED: protein floor still hit with boring defaults; one or two shortened sessions that keep squat/hinge/push/pull patterns alive; steps as walking-with-life instead of a punished treadmill penance; LINK message that says “bad week, MED on, full LINE resumes Monday.” That’s not softness. That’s how twelve-month bodies are built by people who don’t live in a content house.

## Month mapping — numbers as illustration, not destiny

Suppose you start with a soft midsection and average adherence historically around 50%. Months 1–3 aim for adherence 80%+ and a clear waist/photo trend while strength mostly holds. Months 4–6 continue STRIP until side photos stop arguing — maybe you’re ready to transition, maybe you need more strip time; criteria decide. Months 7–9, if earned, push LIFT: food up, same template, chase overload, weak-point work limited to one or two movements. Months 10–12 compare to month one, sketch Year 2, keep LINK. If at month 8 you’re still soft, you didn’t “fail LIFT” — you simply haven’t met LIFT criteria yet. Stay honest. Stay on LINE. The calendar is a guide; the photo and the waist are the vote.
